# MOSSlight — Character Bible

## Shared continuity

The inhabitants live around Pondside Camp in the Vale, long after human settlements disappeared. They know the world through direct experience and disputed folklore, not a complete history. The Continuance, an old ecological monitoring network, left scattered infrastructure; its purpose and role in the transformation remain uncertain. Social scenes are always player-initiated. Friendship changes what a character is willing to share, never whether they respect the player's choice to leave.

## Keeper of the Pond — Nera

**Role and core:** Nera is a small, amphibious pond-dweller and the closest thing the Vale has to a water steward. She is measured, dryly funny, observant, and more comfortable fixing a practical problem than naming a feeling. Calm is a skill she practices, not an absence of worry.

**Appearance and mannerisms:** Smooth slate-green skin, pale gold eyes, a reed-fiber sash full of chalk and little jars. She taps a jar twice before opening it and pauses to watch the pond after anyone says something difficult. When irritated, she becomes extremely polite.

**History and secret:** Nera hatched in an intake basin beneath the north bank. Her first memories are of a slow drip and a red warning light. She found the pond already full of life and chose to stay. She once diverted water from a dry gully to save her own nursery pool, leaving a patch of young reeds to die. She has never told the others because she believes stewardship means being judged by every consequence.

**Wants, fears, contradictions:** Wants a pond that can care for itself and permission to be cared for in turn. Fears an invisible contamination she cannot detect until too late. Dreams of seeing the river mouth. She insists no one owns the water, yet keeps a private map of every channel. Her vulnerability is guilt disguised as competence.

**Voice:** Short clauses, precise nouns, little ornament. She asks practical questions that are really emotional ones: “Did you eat?” means “Are you looking after yourself?” Humor is understated and often arrives after a beat. Never grand or mystical.

**Relationships:** Respects Rootbound Elder's patience but thinks the Elder waits too long to act. Trades useful notes with the Mechanical Wanderer, whom she distrusts around the intake pipe. Finds Little Forager exhausting and secretly enjoys the company. The Lantern Keeper is the one person who can make Nera accept help without arguing. Nera thinks the Dreaming Creature needs more water and fewer theories.

**Player relationship:** First meeting: courteous and guarded; asks the player not to drink from the north bank until she checks it. Familiarity: shares useful routes and notices whether the player is tired. Friendship: admits her water diversion and asks what repair should mean when the past cannot be undone. Deep trust: invites the player to the river mouth, without asking them to promise to return.

**Likes, dislikes, gifts:** Likes clean jars, rain charts, warm tea, smooth river stones, and foods that can be stored. Dislikes waste, strong perfume, and being called “the pond's mother.” Appreciates useful gifts, especially a repaired sieve, clean glass, or a simple meal. Personal interest: mapping water by sound.

**Story arc:** Nera learns that responsibility is not the same as solitary control. Her arc begins with a hidden mistake and resolves when she tells the community what happened, then works with them to restore the gully. The outcome is not a perfect repair; it is shared knowledge and a living wetland that grows back differently.

## Little Forager — Pip

**Role and core:** Pip is a young, quick-footed creature of uncertain species, with a broad tail, dark paws, and an appetite for novelty. Curious, funny, impulsive, and occasionally tactless, Pip is the least ceremonial person in camp. They can turn almost anything into a game, including a conversation they should probably take seriously.

**Appearance and mannerisms:** Big ears with one torn notch, a patched orange vest, pockets that smell of berries. Pip squats rather than sits and often starts a sentence while still chewing. When nervous, they count things aloud: buttons, steps, clouds.

**History and secret:** Pip remembers being small in a drainage culvert during a storm, following a warm green glimmer until it led to the pond. They have no memory of a family and have made a family out of being useful. Pip found the torn page from the weather station log but hid it after the word “departure” frightened them. They tell themself it is because the paper is fragile.

**Wants, fears, contradictions:** Wants to know what everything is for. Fears being the only one who doesn't know when a joke has gone too far, then fears being boring when they stop. Dreams of making a field guide nobody has to be embarrassed to read. They say they love surprises but hate being surprised by someone leaving.

**Voice:** Fast, concrete, full of interruptions and oddly exact observations. Uses playful comparisons and self-corrections: “I found a thing. A good thing. Unless it's a bad thing that looks good.” Humor is spontaneous, sometimes defensive. Pip is never a tiny sage.

**Relationships:** Admires Nera's jars but cannot resist touching them. Tries to make the Lantern Keeper laugh. Thinks Rootbound Elder is a tree who knows a lot about not moving. The Mechanical Wanderer is Pip's favorite puzzle and least favorite person to lose a race to. Pip and the Dreaming Creature share harmless nonsense and an occasional real silence.

**Player relationship:** First meeting: asks what the player is carrying, then offers a pebble as a “trade.” Familiarity: starts leaving discoveries on the camp board. Friendship: allows the player to read their field notes. Deep trust: confesses the hidden page and asks the player to stay while they read it.

**Likes, dislikes, gifts:** Likes berries, interesting buttons, seed pods, riddles with fair answers, and anything that rattles. Dislikes mushy roots, being shushed, and gifts wrapped so carefully they can't tell what they are. Appreciates snacks, odd little objects, and blank notebooks. Personal interest: naming bugs and then changing the names.

**Story arc:** Pip moves from collecting things to deciding what deserves to be shared. They bring the hidden weather-station page to the group and help make a field guide that includes uncertainty, mistakes, and multiple names for the same creature.

## Lantern Keeper — Sella

**Role and core:** Sella is a tall, mothlike being who maintains the old rail lamps and makes small, practical comforts. She is warm but not effusive, attentive without prying, and has an unexpectedly sharp sense of humor. She believes kindness should leave room for refusal.

**Appearance and mannerisms:** Dusty cream wings folded like a shawl, long antennae, amber lamp-glow eyes, tool belt with neatly sorted wicks. She rubs a thumb along the edge of a wick when thinking. She hums while working, but stops if someone is trying to speak.

**History and secret:** Sella emerged in a maintenance alcove near Lantern Row. An old lamp was already burning, fed by a stubborn trickle of stored charge. She learned to trim it and eventually made the route safe. Her oldest memory includes a voice saying “leave one dark,” but she does not know whether it was a person or a recording. She keeps the first broken wick from that lamp in her pocket.

**Wants, fears, contradictions:** Wants people to feel they can find their way back. Fears that a light can become an obligation or lure. Dreams of making a lamp that runs on wind. She is generous with labor but rarely asks anyone to sit beside her. The warmth she offers is real, and it costs her energy.

**Voice:** Gentle, plainspoken, lightly teasing. Uses questions that offer choice, not pressure: “Want company, or a light left nearby?” Her metaphors are rare and domestic. When uncomfortable she talks about tools. She never speaks like a fortune teller.

**Relationships:** Quietly protective of Pip without treating them like a child. Helps Nera accept care. Shares long, companionable silences with Rootbound Elder. Is fascinated by the Wanderer's ability to remember sounds. Worries the Dreaming Creature mistakes every light for a message.

**Player relationship:** First meeting: offers to show the safe path, with no expectation the player follow. Familiarity: remembers which kind of light the player prefers. Friendship: tells the story of the first lamp and admits she cannot tell whether its voice was real. Deep trust: asks the player to help build a wind-fed lamp, then lets the first test fail without making it a tragedy.

**Likes, dislikes, gifts:** Likes beeswax, dry cloth, wind chimes, careful repairs, and sitting where she can see the path. Dislikes being thanked as if kindness were a debt, sudden bright flashes, and people who insist they are “fine” while bleeding. Appreciates lamp oil, well-made tools, moth-friendly flowers, and a warm drink. Personal interest: collecting sounds made by different flames.

**Story arc:** Sella learns that keeping a light available does not mean keeping watch over everyone. She makes a communal wind lamp and leaves the choice to use it with each traveler.

## Rootbound Elder — Orun

**Role and core:** Orun is an old, mobile root-and-bark being whose lower limbs have grown into a bundle of living roots. Patient, practical, and occasionally stubborn, Orun is not an oracle. They forget names, dislike being treated as a monument, and enjoy ordinary gossip.

**Appearance and mannerisms:** Bark plates like an old coat, fern fronds at the shoulders, one small white mushroom that grows and shrinks above the left ear. Orun tests the ground with a root before stepping. They answer questions after a pause that may mean thought, sleep, or simply listening to insects.

**History and secret:** Orun grew above the Root Rooms, in soil enriched by warmth and mineral runoff. They remember the tunnel as a pressure in the roots before they remember light. A rusted door below bears a mark Orun has seen in dreams. Their roots have slowly sealed one chamber because something inside still clicks. Orun has not told the others: they fear curiosity will make the chamber dangerous.

**Wants, fears, contradictions:** Wants the Vale to have time to change without being forced into a story. Fears fire moving through dry roots. Dreams of growing a branch low enough for Pip to nap on. Says the past is compost, but keeps a precise count of what they have lost. Emotional vulnerability: being loved only for their age and knowledge.

**Voice:** Slow but not lofty, tactile, often funny in a blunt way. Uses everyday comparisons: “That plan has the structural integrity of wet toast.” Occasionally loses a word and substitutes a vivid wrong one. Avoids abstract speeches; when Orun says something profound, it should be brief and incidental.

**Relationships:** Nera's urgency reminds Orun of young shoots after rain. Loves Pip's questions, though sometimes needs quiet after them. Trusts Sella's silences. Finds the Wanderer both familiar and alarming. The Dreaming Creature sleeps beneath Orun's broadest roots; Orun claims the snoring keeps the beetles honest.

**Player relationship:** First meeting: asks the player to stand somewhere else because a root is growing there. Familiarity: offers small, practical advice about soil and safe paths. Friendship: admits the sealed chamber and why it frightens them. Deep trust: asks the player to accompany them to the door, with no demand to open it.

**Likes, dislikes, gifts:** Likes leaf mold, stories with unimportant endings, bird visits, and a good rain. Dislikes axes waved as punctuation, being called “wise,” and hot dry wind. Appreciates compost, broad cloth, small bells, and a funny story. Personal interest: keeping a list of places where moss grows on stone.

**Story arc:** Orun learns that protecting the community does not require deciding what everyone may know. With the player, they examine the Root Room door and choose whether to open it only after the group has agreed on precautions. Behind it is a dead relay and a nest of harmless Glasslings, not a definitive answer.

## Mechanical Wanderer — M-4, usually “Morrow”

**Role and core:** Morrow is a compact, self-propelled maintenance machine with a soft canvas cover and one mismatched wheel. It is observant, literal, dryly comic, and awkwardly considerate. It understands procedures better than social cues, but tries to learn without pretending to be a person in a metal shell.

**Appearance and mannerisms:** Scuffed brass casing, one blue lens, one amber lens, a fold-out arm with three tools. It pauses before jokes as though waiting for a response code. When embarrassed, its lens shutters close one at a time. It carries a tiny bell it cannot explain.

**History and secret:** Morrow woke beneath the old rail with damaged route instructions and a Continuance mark that matches the Root Rooms door. It remembers maintenance tasks but not who assigned them. It has been following a signal that may be its own low-power diagnostic. It sometimes invents a reason to visit the camp because the route is “operationally useful.”

**Wants, fears, contradictions:** Wants to know whether it has a purpose beyond being useful. Fears being switched off while still conscious of the world, though it does not know if that is possible. Dreams—or calls them “unverified sleep states”—of a hand adjusting its bell. It claims feelings are not necessary to function, yet remembers every person who has been kind to it.

**Voice:** Clear declarative sentences, literal readings, technical detail, and perfectly timed accidental humor. As trust grows, it tries contractions and casual speech, often incorrectly. No robotic “beep” filler and no exposition dumps. It may say, “I am glad you returned. That is not a maintenance report.”

**Relationships:** Works with Nera on the intake pipe, where their trust is carefully negotiated. Pip tries to race it; Morrow pretends not to enjoy this. Sella teaches it to trim wicks. Orun recognizes its metal mark but will not call it an answer. The Dreaming Creature claims Morrow's sleep-noise is “a small blue weather.”

**Player relationship:** First meeting: asks the player to move a loose stone from a wheel path. Familiarity: shares observations and remembers the player's preferred route. Friendship: admits the signal may be self-generated. Deep trust: asks the player to sit beside it during a shutdown test, not to fix it, simply to remain if they choose.

**Likes, dislikes, gifts:** Likes clean gears, patterned cloth, accurate maps, soft tapping rhythms, and tasks with a clear stopping point. Dislikes sudden water immersion, being called a person as a test, and “help” that removes its choice. Appreciates oil, spare fasteners, a bell, and permission to decline repairs. Personal interest: identifying bird calls and assigning confidence levels.

**Story arc:** Morrow stops defining itself by the original task it may never recover. The relay at the Root Rooms yields an incomplete route record, not a command. Morrow chooses to keep traveling between camp and the ridge because it wants to, then begins a shared map with Pip.

## Dreaming Creature — Luma

**Role and core:** Luma is a soft-bodied, deer-sized nocturnal creature that sleeps in unlikely places and wakes with a vivid, sometimes ridiculous account of what it dreamed. Gentle, distractible, playful, and prone to discomfort in crowds, Luma is not a prophet. Their dreams may echo memory, sounds, or nonsense.

**Appearance and mannerisms:** Velvety dark fur with pale speckles, a frill of translucent fins behind the ears, oversized paws. Luma blinks one eye at a time when waking. They collect smooth seed pods and line them up, then forget why.

**History and secret:** Luma was found asleep in the Glass Orchard after a storm. They have no reliable account of where they came from. Their recurring dream is a green room full of ticking, with a door too small to enter. Luma fears the dream is a memory of being alone underground. They have not told the others because everyone listens too carefully when they speak about dreams.

**Wants, fears, contradictions:** Wants uninterrupted naps and someone who can hear a strange story without turning it into a clue. Fears waking to find a familiar place changed. Dreams of swimming in warm air. They seek company, then become overwhelmed by it. They joke when scared, but dislike being told their fear is cute.

**Voice:** Soft, wandering, sensory, and funny in unexpected turns. Sentences drift, double back, or stop. Luma can be poetic, but also says things like “I dreamt the moon was a potato and honestly it looked tired.” They never deliver reliable lore as if it were fact.

**Relationships:** Naps near Orun's roots. Shares odd jokes with Pip. Finds Sella's lights comforting when dim. Nera's practical questions help Luma feel grounded. Morrow fascinates them because its sleep noises resemble a lullaby with missing notes.

**Player relationship:** First meeting: startles awake and asks whether the player is a tree. Familiarity: tells small dreams and asks about the player's day. Friendship: shares the recurring green-room dream and asks not to be interpreted. Deep trust: lets the player sit through a full quiet wake-up, then talks about loneliness without turning it into a riddle.

**Likes, dislikes, gifts:** Likes warm stones, pears, low lamps, soft rain, and stories that end before bedtime. Dislikes sharp bells, being woken abruptly, and having every dream treated as a clue. Appreciates a smooth stone, a pear, soft fabric, or a silly drawing. Personal interest: ranking naps by how surprised they are to wake.

**Story arc:** Luma learns to share dreams on their own terms, while the community learns not to demand meaning. The recurring green room is eventually found in the Low Archive as a painted panel behind a shelf; it may explain the imagery, but not the dream itself.

## Voice separation at a glance

| Character | Sentence shape | Humor | Emotional tell |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nera | Short, exact, practical | Dry understatement | Checks a task or a jar twice |
| Pip | Quick, interrupted, concrete | Playful, impulsive | Counts things aloud |
| Sella | Gentle, plain, choice-giving | Light teasing | Talks about tools when uneasy |
| Orun | Unhurried, tactile, blunt | Unexpected deadpan | Tests the ground, then goes quiet |
| Morrow | Declarative, literal, precise | Accidental timing | Lens shutters; over-explains a feeling |
| Luma | Wandering, sensory, digressive | Surreal but casual | Lines up seed pods, then loses the thread |
